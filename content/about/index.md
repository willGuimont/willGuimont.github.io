## What I work on

I'm interested in how robots perceive the world, and how we can build models that hold up outside the conditions they were trained in. My research explores using additional sensor modalities, such as hyperspectral, polar, and LiDAR data, during training to learn more robust and generalizable representations.

I also build software to explore ideas: graphics experiments, algorithm visualizations, electronics and robotics libraries, and small apps and games. My interests span computer science, mathematics, computer graphics, and AI. You can find the work on my [Projects](@/projects/_index.md) and [Papers](@/papers/index.md) pages.

## Selected experience

### Research

At Norlab, I've worked on perception using point clouds, forest imagery, and robot sensor data. For my master's research, I introduced <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01864" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>MaskBEV</strong></a>, an approach to detecting objects and completing their footprints in bird's-eye-view 3D point clouds. I've also contributed to tree segmentation and species classification with <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09458" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>SilvaScenes</strong></a>, and terrain classification with <a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS58592.2024.10801407" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>BorealTC</strong></a>.

### Teaching

I've been a teaching assistant in <a href="https://github.com/ulaval-damas/glo4030-labs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">deep learning</a>, <a href="https://github.com/norlab-ulaval/glo4001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">mobile robotics</a>, operating systems, and software engineering at Université Laval. I design laboratories and teaching material as well as support students. In Winter 2025, I took responsibility for the deep learning course apart from lectures.

I also developed workshops on [Bash](@/blog/2025-09-06-bash-workshop/index.md), [Docker](@/blog/2025-09-07-docker-workshop/index.md), [testing, refactoring, and test-driven development](@/blog/2021-10-11-glo2003-workshops/index.md). I like teaching principles that remain useful as the tools change. The workshop approach is described in <a href="https://doi.org/10.24908/pceea.2025.19722" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Teaching Software Development Skills</a>.

### Industry

My work has included computer vision and automated production lines at <a href="https://www.devoray.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Devoray</strong></a>, ultrasonic-data analysis at <a href="https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Olympus NDT Canada</strong></a>, and computer vision and photogrammetry at <a href="https://www.bentley.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Bentley Systems</strong></a>. At Bentley, I developed an algorithm for detecting and modelling aerial cables from images, which contributed to a <a href="https://patents.google.com/patent/US11521357B2/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">U.S. patent</a>.

### Robotics mentoring

I've mentored students in robotics and programming with <a href="https://www.facebook.com/chevaliersfirst/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Team 5440 Les Chevaliers</strong></a> at École secondaire de La Seigneurie since 2016. The team competed in <a href="https://www.firstinspires.org/robotics/frc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FIRST Robotics Competition</a> through 2025 and competes in <a href="https://robo-crc.ca/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CRC Robotics</a> from the 2026 season onward. Teaching and building robots with students have been a substantial part of my life alongside university research.
